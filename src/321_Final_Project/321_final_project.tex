\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

This report aims to characterize both the longitudinal and lateral linear stability of the A-7A Corsair II which is
critical in determining the flying qualities of the aircraft as well as the creation of a generalized plant for controls.
Furthermore, aircraft designers can use stability analysis to improve the static and dynamic stability of the aircraft
through modifying certain features such as control surface sizing and placement to meet the design requirements of the
aircraft.

\part*{Longitudinal Stability Derivatives}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{Longitudinal Stability Derivatives}

The longitudinal stability derivatives are useful in determining the characteristics of the aircraft in Straight, Level,
Unaccelerated Flight (SLUF) along its central fuselage axis.
These quantities enable the understanding of how the aircraft tends to respond longitudinally towards perturbations
which are common in most flight regimes.

\section{Longitudinal Nondimensionalization Conventions}

The target linearized equations are built upon dimensional derivatives which is not useful during the design process of
an aircraft.
Instead, nondimensional derivatives are utilized which must be converted via these three equations defined as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_1}
C_X = \frac{X}{\qbar S}, \qquad
C_Z = \frac{Z}{\qbar S}, \qquad
C_m = \frac{M}{\qbar S \cbar},
\end{equation}

together with the nondimensional perturbation variables

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_2}
\hat{u} = \frac{u}{U_1}, \qquad
\hat{\alpha} = \alpha, \qquad
\hat{\dot\alpha} = \frac{\dot\alpha\,\cbar}{2U_1}, \qquad
\hat{q} = \frac{q\cbar}{2U_1}, \qquad
\hat{\delta}_e = \delta_e .
\end{equation}

These derivatives are taken about the trim state (subscript $1$), where $U = U_1$
and $\qbar = \qbar_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho_1 U_1^2$.

\section{X-Force Derivatives}

The total axial force represents the combined effects from both the thrust and the associated drag of the aircraft and
is formulated as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_3}
X = \qbar S\,C_X = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho U^2 S\,(-C_D + C_T),
\end{equation}

where $-C_D$ reflects drag acting opposite to the body $x$-axis and $C_T$ is the
thrust coefficient.

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $u$}

Differentiating with respect to $u$ at trim results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_4}
\dd{X}{u} = \rho_1 U_1 S\,(-C_{D_1}) + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho_1 U_1^2 S\left(-\dd{C_D}{u}\right).
\end{equation}

Using $\hat u = u/U_1$, the chain rule gives $\partial C_D/\partial u = C_{D_u}/U_1$.
Substituting and factoring $\qbar_1 S/(mU_1)$ results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_5}
\boxed{\;X_u = \frac{1}{m}\dd{X}{u} = -\frac{\qbar_1 S}{mU_1}\bigl(C_{D_u} + 2C_{D_1}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $\alpha$}

At fixed $U_1$, only $C_D$ and $C_L$ depend on $\alpha$.
Utilizing the small-angle approximation where $\sin\alpha \approx \alpha$ results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_6}
X = \qbar_1 S\,(-C_D + C_L\,\alpha + \cdots).
\end{equation}

Differentiating and evaluating at trim results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_7}
\boxed{\;X_\alpha = \frac{1}{m}\dd{X}{\alpha} = -\frac{\qbar_1 S}{m}\bigl(C_{D_\alpha} - C_{L_1}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $\delta_e$}

Only the elevon-induced drag contributes and results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_8}
\boxed{\;X_{\delta_e} = -\frac{\qbar_1 S}{m}C_{D_{\delta_e}}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Thrust derivative $X_{T_u}$}

Similarly to $X_u$, the thrust term $+C_T$ differentiated results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_9}
\boxed{\;X_{T_u} = \frac{\qbar_1 S}{mU_1}\bigl(C_{T_{X_u}} + 2C_{T_{X_1}}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conversion between $\alpha$ and $w$}

Since $w = U_1\sin\alpha \approx U_1\alpha$, the chain rule results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_10}
X_w = \dd{(X/m)}{w} = \frac{1}{U_1}\dd{(X/m)}{\alpha}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;X_\alpha = U_1 X_w\;}
\end{equation}

\section{Z-Force Derivatives}

The total vertical force represents the combined effects from both the lift of the aircraft and its weight which
is formulated as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_11}
Z = -\qbar S\,C_L \quad
\end{equation}

assuming the $C_{D} \sin\alpha$ term is neglected via the small-angle approximation.

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $u$}

Differentiating and evaluating at trim results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_12}
\dd{Z}{u} = -\rho_1 U_1 S\,C_{L_1} - \tfrac{1}{2}\rho_1 U_1^2 S\,\frac{C_{L_u}}{U_1},
\end{equation}

canceling terms results in the final form

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_13}
\boxed{\;Z_u = -\frac{\qbar_1 S}{mU_1}\bigl(C_{L_u} + 2C_{L_1}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}

The resultant structure mirrors $X_u$, but with $C_L$ instead of $C_D$.

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $\alpha$}

The body-axis $Z$-force depends on both $C_L$ and the $\alpha$-projection of $C_D$ which results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_14}
Z = -\qbar_1 S\bigl(C_L + C_D\,\alpha\bigr).
\end{equation}

Differentiating with respect to $\alpha$ and evaluating at trim results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_15}
\boxed{\;Z_\alpha = -\frac{\qbar_1 S}{m}\bigl(C_{L_\alpha} + C_{D_1}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivatives with respect to $\dot\alpha$ and $q$}

These are due to the unsteady aerodynamic terms.
The nondimensional derivatives use $\hat{\dot\alpha} = \dot\alpha\,\cbar/(2U_1)$ and $\hat q = q\cbar/(2U_1)$, so
converting back to dimensional $\dot\alpha$ and $q$ introduces a factor $\cbar/(2U_1)$, resulting in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_16}
\boxed{\;Z_{\dot\alpha} = \frac{1}{m}\dd{Z}{\dot\alpha}
       = -\frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar}{2mU_1}C_{L_{\dot\alpha}}\;}
\end{equation}

and similarly

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_17}
\boxed{\;Z_q = \frac{1}{m}\dd{Z}{q}
       = -\frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar}{2mU_1}C_{L_q}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $\delta_e$}

$\delta_{e}$ solely depends on $C_L$ which results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_18}
\boxed{\;Z_{\delta_e} = -\frac{\qbar_1 S}{m}C_{L_{\delta_e}}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conversion to $w$-derivatives}

By applying the same chain rule logic as for $X$, the result is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_19}
\boxed{\;Z_\alpha = U_1 Z_w, \qquad Z_{\dot\alpha} = U_1 Z_{\dot w}\;}
\end{equation}

\section{M-Pitching Moment Derivatives}

The total longitudinal pitching moment represents the resultant lift and pitching effects from all control surfaces
which is formulated as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_20}
M = \qbar S \cbar\,C_m
\end{equation}

with the dimension derivative as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_21}
M_{(\cdot)} = (1/\Iyy)\,\partial M/\partial(\cdot)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $u$}

Both $\qbar$ and $C_m$ depend on $u$ and following the same expansion as for $X_u$ results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_22}
\dd{M}{u} = \rho_1 U_1 S\cbar\,C_{m_1} + \qbar_1 S\cbar\,\frac{C_{m_u}}{U_1},
\end{equation}

and subsequently

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_23}
\boxed{\;M_u = \frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar}{\Iyy U_1}\bigl(C_{m_u} + 2C_{m_1}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}

In SLUF, $C_{m_1} = 0$ but the formula retains the term for generality.

\subsection{Derivatives with respect to $\alpha$ and $\delta_e$}

Both $\alpha$ and $\delta_{e}$ are static terms which results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_24}
\boxed{\;M_\alpha = \frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar}{\Iyy}C_{m_\alpha}\;}
\end{equation}

and similarly

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_25}
\boxed{\;M_{\delta_e} = \frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar}{\Iyy}C_{m_{\delta_e}}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivatives with respect to $\dot\alpha$ and $q$}

Both $\dot\alpha$ and $q$ involve the unsteady-aerodynamic nondimensionalization $\cbar/(2U_1)$.
However, $M$ already carries one factor of $\cbar$ from its own coefficient
definition, so the dimensional derivative ends up with $\cbar^{\,2}$ and results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_26}
\boxed{\;M_{\dot\alpha} = \frac{1}{\Iyy}\dd{M}{\dot\alpha}
       = \frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar^{\,2}}{2\Iyy U_1}C_{m_{\dot\alpha}}\;}
\end{equation}

and similarly

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_27}
\boxed{\;M_q = \frac{1}{\Iyy}\dd{M}{q}
       = \frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar^{\,2}}{2\Iyy U_1}C_{m_q}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Thrust-pitching derivatives}

Applying the same logic as $X_{T_u}$ to the moment results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_28}
\boxed{\;M_{T_u} = \frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar}{\Iyy U_1}\bigl(C_{m_{T_u}} + 2C_{m_{T_1}}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}

and similarly

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_29}
\boxed{\;M_{T_\alpha} = \frac{\qbar_1 S\cbar}{\Iyy}C_{m_{T_\alpha}}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conversion to $w$-derivatives}

By applying the same chain rule logic as for $X$ and $Z$ for the moment, the result is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_30}
\boxed{\;M_\alpha = U_1 M_w, \qquad M_{\dot\alpha} = U_1 M_{\dot w}\;}
\end{equation}

\part*{Lateral-Directional Stability Derivatives}
\addcontentsline{toc}{part}{Lateral-Directional Stability Derivatives}

Similarly to the longitudinal stability derivatives, the lateral stability derivatives are useful in determining the
characteristics of the aircraft in SLUF along the vertical and lateral axes.
These quantities enable the understanding of how the aircraft tends to respond in more complex modes with any
combination of lateral and directional contributions dependent on the specific mode.

\section{Lateral-Directional Nondimensionalization Conventions}

As before with the longitudinal derivatives, the target linearized equations are built upon dimensional derivatives
which is not useful during the design process.
Instead, the following nondimensional derivative conversions are required to translate the design into the
stability analysis regime

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_31}
C_y = \frac{Y}{\qbar S}, \qquad
C_\ell = \frac{L}{\qbar S b}, \qquad
C_n = \frac{N}{\qbar S b},
\end{equation}

where $L$ denotes the rolling moment (not lift) and $b$ is the wingspan.
The important distinction from the longitudinal case is that the reference length is not the chord~$\bar c$, but
rather the span~$b$ because rolling and yawing moments scale with the span.

The lateral-directional perturbation variables are

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_32}
\hat\beta = \beta, \qquad
\hat p = \frac{p\,b}{2U_1}, \qquad
\hat r = \frac{r\,b}{2U_1}, \qquad
\hat{\delta}_a = \delta_a, \qquad
\hat{\delta}_r = \delta_r
\end{equation}

All derivatives are evaluated at the symmetric trim state, where
$\beta_1 = p_1 = r_1 = 0$, and consequently $C_{y_1} = C_{\ell_1} = C_{n_1} = 0$.
This eliminates the $+2C_{(\cdot)_1}$ kinematic term which appeared in the longitudinal $u$-derivatives, and it also
means lateral-directional derivatives are decoupled from the speed perturbations.

\section{Y-Force (Side-Force) Derivatives}

The total side force is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_33}
Y = \qbar S\,C_y
\end{equation}

Due to $Y$ already lying on along the stability $y$-axis and the lateral trim coefficients vanishing, there is no
analog of the $C_{L_1}$ or $C_{D_1}$ correction terms that appeared in $X_\alpha$ and $Z_\alpha$.
Therefore, each of the nondimensional coefficients map directly to its respective dimensional derivative.

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $\beta$}

Since $\hat\beta = \beta$ requires no rescaling, this results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_34}
\dd{Y}{\beta} = \qbar_1 S\,C_{y_\beta},
\end{equation}

and consequently

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_35}
\boxed{\;Y_\beta = \frac{1}{m}\dd{Y}{\beta} = \frac{\qbar_1 S}{m}C_{y_\beta}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $p$}

The nondimensional roll rate is $\hat p = pb/(2U_1)$, and therefore
$\partial C_y/\partial p = \bigl(b/(2U_1)\bigr) C_{y_p}$.
This then results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_36}
\dd{Y}{p} = \qbar_1 S\,\frac{b}{2U_1}\,C_{y_p},
\end{equation}

and consequently

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_37}
\boxed{\;Y_p = \frac{1}{m}\dd{Y}{p} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{2mU_1}C_{y_p}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $r$}

Similar to the roll rate, scaling with $\hat r = rb/(2U_1)$ results in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_38}
\boxed{\;Y_r = \frac{1}{m}\dd{Y}{r} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{2mU_1}C_{y_r}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Control derivatives}

The aileron and rudder deflections are not rescaled which results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_39}
\boxed{\;Y_{\delta_a} = \frac{\qbar_1 S}{m}C_{y_{\delta_a}}\;}
\qquad
\boxed{\;Y_{\delta_r} = \frac{\qbar_1 S}{m}C_{y_{\delta_r}}\;}
\end{equation}

\section{L-Rolling Moment Derivatives}

The rolling moment is defined as $L = \qbar S b\,C_\ell$ and the dimensional derivative is
$L_{(\cdot)} = (1/\Ixx)\,\partial L/\partial(\cdot)$.

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $\beta$}

Simply differentiates to

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_40}
\dd{L}{\beta} = \qbar_1 S b\,C_{\ell_\beta},
\end{equation}

which dimensionalizes to

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_41}
\boxed{\;L_\beta = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{\Ixx}C_{\ell_\beta}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $p$}

The rate-derivative carries a factor $b/(2U_1)$ from $\hat p$ which results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_42}
\dd{L}{p} = \qbar_1 S b\,\frac{b}{2U_1}\,C_{\ell_p}
         = \frac{\qbar_1 S b^{\,2}}{2U_1}C_{\ell_p},
\end{equation}

and consequently

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_43}
\boxed{\;L_p = \frac{1}{\Ixx}\dd{L}{p} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b^{\,2}}{2\Ixx U_1}C_{\ell_p}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $r$}

Same structure as above results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_44}
\boxed{\;L_r = \frac{\qbar_1 S b^{\,2}}{2\Ixx U_1}C_{\ell_r}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Control derivatives}

As before with the side force derivatives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_45}
\boxed{\;L_{\delta_a} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{\Ixx}C_{\ell_{\delta_a}}\;}
\qquad
\boxed{\;L_{\delta_r} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{\Ixx}C_{\ell_{\delta_r}}\;}
\end{equation}

\section{N-Yawing Moment Derivatives}

The yawing moment is defined as $N = \qbar S b\,C_n$ and the dimensional derivative is
$N_{(\cdot)} = (1/\Izz)\,\partial N/\partial(\cdot)$.
The structure mirrors the rolling-moment derivatives exactly, with $\Izz$ replacing $\Ixx$ and $C_n$ replacing $C_\ell$.

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $\beta$}

Simply differentiates to

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_46}
\boxed{\;N_\beta = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{\Izz}C_{n_\beta}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Thrust-induced derivative $N_{T_\beta}$}

Unique to the yawing moment, the $N_{T_\beta}$ thrust coefficient differentiated results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_47}
\boxed{\;N_{T_\beta} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{\Izz}C_{n_{T_\beta}}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $p$}

Similarly to $L_p$, differentiating results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_48}
\boxed{\;N_p = \frac{1}{\Izz}\dd{N}{p}
       = \frac{\qbar_1 S b^{\,2}}{2\Izz U_1}C_{n_p}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivative with respect to $r$}

Similarly to $L_r$, differentiating results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_49}
\boxed{\;N_r = \frac{1}{\Izz}\dd{N}{r}
       = \frac{\qbar_1 S b^{\,2}}{2\Izz U_1}C_{n_r}\;}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Control derivatives}

Finally, the control derivatives match the rolling moment derivatives which results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_50}
\boxed{\;N_{\delta_a} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{\Izz}C_{n_{\delta_a}}\;}
\qquad
\boxed{\;N_{\delta_r} = \frac{\qbar_1 S b}{\Izz}C_{n_{\delta_r}}\;}
\end{equation}

\section{Linearized Perturbation Equations in Matrix Form}

\subsection{Longitudinal Equations}

The longitudinal perturbation dynamics, written in the classical state-space form of
$\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A_\text{long}\,\mathbf{x} + B_\text{long}\,\mathbf{u}$ with a
state vector of $\mathbf{x} = [\Delta u,\,\Delta w,\,\Delta q,\,\Delta\theta]^\top$
and a control vector of $\mathbf{u} = [\Delta\delta_e,\,\Delta\delta_T]^\top$, are shown as

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_51}
\begin{bmatrix} \Delta\dot u \\ \Delta\dot w \\ \Delta\dot q \\ \Delta\dot\theta \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
X_u                          & X_w                          & 0                          & -g \\
Z_u                          & Z_w                          & U_1                        & 0 \\
M_u + M_{\dot w} Z_u         & M_w + M_{\dot w} Z_w         & M_q + M_{\dot w} U_1       & 0 \\
0                            & 0                            & 1                          & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \Delta u \\ \Delta w \\ \Delta q \\ \Delta\theta \end{bmatrix}
+
\begin{bmatrix}
X_{\delta_e}                  & X_{\delta_T} \\
Z_{\delta_e}                  & Z_{\delta_T} \\
M_{\delta_e} + M_{\dot w} Z_{\delta_e} & M_{\delta_T} + M_{\dot w} Z_{\delta_T} \\
0                             & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \Delta\delta_e \\ \Delta\delta_T \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

Using the values previously found for the dimensional derivatives results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_52}
A_\text{long} =
\begin{bmatrix}
-9.418\!\times\!10^{-4} & -0.04918 & 0       & -32.17 \\
-0.0225                 & -1.162   & 634.4   & 0      \\
+1.048\!\times\!10^{-3} & -0.01400 & -0.8260 & 0      \\
0                       & 0        & 1       & 0
\end{bmatrix} and
\qquad
B_\text{long} =
\begin{bmatrix}
6.971  & 0 \\
-97.93 & 0 \\
-18.84 & 0 \\
0      & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

The throttle column of $B_\text{long}$ is zero because of the assumed lack of perturbations to the throttles
based on the constant thrust assumption which results in $X_{T_u} = M_{T_u} = M_{T_\alpha} = 0$.

\subsection{Lateral-Directional Equations}

The lateral-directional perturbation equations take the general form of
$\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A_\text{lat}\,\mathbf{x} + B_\text{lat}\,\mathbf{u}$ with a
state vector of $\mathbf{x} = [\Delta\beta,\,\Delta p,\,\Delta r,\,\Delta\phi]^\top$
and a control vector of $\mathbf{u} = [\Delta\delta_a,\,\Delta\delta_r]^\top$

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_53}
\begin{bmatrix} \Delta\dot\beta \\ \Delta\dot p \\ \Delta\dot r \\ \Delta\dot\phi \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
Y_\beta/U_1 & Y_p/U_1 & -(1 - Y_r/U_1) & g\cos\theta_1/U_1 \\
L'_\beta    & L'_p    & L'_r           & 0 \\
N'_\beta    & N'_p    & N'_r           & 0 \\
0           & 1       & 0              & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \Delta\beta \\ \Delta p \\ \Delta r \\ \Delta\phi \end{bmatrix}
+
\begin{bmatrix}
0           & Y_{\delta_r}/U_1 \\
L'_{\delta_a} & L'_{\delta_r} \\
N'_{\delta_a} & N'_{\delta_r} \\
0           & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \Delta\delta_a \\ \Delta\delta_r \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

To account for and simplify the $I_{xz}$ moment coupling, primed derivatives are used as shown here

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_54}
L'_K = \frac{L_K + (\Ixz/\Ixx)\,N_K}{1 - \Ixz^{\,2}/(\Ixx\,\Izz)},
\qquad
N'_K = \frac{N_K + (\Ixz/\Izz)\,L_K}{1 - \Ixz^{\,2}/(\Ixx\,\Izz)},
\qquad K \in \{\beta, p, r, \delta_a, \delta_r\}
\end{equation}

Similarly, using the values found prior results in

\begin{equation}\label{eq:equation_55}
A_\text{lat} =
\begin{bmatrix}
-0.1871 & 0       & -1     & 0.05059 \\
-27.09  & -2.615  & 0.8582 & 0       \\
3.675   & -0.1135 & -0.5546 & 0      \\
0       & 1       & 0       & 0
\end{bmatrix} and
\qquad
B_\text{lat} =
\begin{bmatrix}
0     & 0.05494 \\
17.87 & 5.171   \\
1.148 & -5.759  \\
0     & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\section{Eigenvalues and Modal Parameters}

Utilizing the numerical $A_{long}$, $A_{lat}$, $B_{long}$, and $B_{lat}$ matrices, the eigenvalues were computed and
associated with the common SLUF aircraft modes of short period, phugoid, dutch roll, roll, and spiral.
All modes are dynamically stable with the real part of the eigenvalues all being negative, resulting in overall
aircraft dynamic stability.
Of further note, the dutch roll mode only has moderate damping compared to expectations which leads to a significantly
longer return time.
